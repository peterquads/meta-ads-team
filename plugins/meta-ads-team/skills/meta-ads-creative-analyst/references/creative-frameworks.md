# Creative Analysis Frameworks

How to read creative the way Peter does. The contrarian correction is up front, because it changes how you score everything below it.

## Hook rate and hold rate are diagnostics, not targets
In a correlation study across $3M+/month over 12 months, hook rate and hold rate showed near-zero correlation with business outcomes. The only creative signal with a meaningful (still modest) tie to CPA was outbound CTR, around -0.17. Hook to CPA landed near 0.06, hook to revenue near -0.12.

So do not chase a hook-rate number. Steer by spend, revenue, audience cost, and frequency. Use hook and hold only to locate WHERE a creative breaks.
- Hook rate = 3-sec views / impressions. Rough bands when you need them: under 25% weak, around 30% passable, over 40% strong. Diagnostic only.
- Hold rate = 15-sec views / 3-sec views.
- Outbound CTR is the creative signal that actually tracks efficiency. Weight it over hook and hold.
- The zones are sequential. You cannot fix a bad hook with a great body, or a bad body with a great CTA. Earn each zone in order. Hook decides if they watch, body if they believe, CTA if they act.

## Cascade Targeting, where the message lives
Segmentation happens in the creative, not Meta's targeting. Three levels, ordered by impact:
- **Level 1, Market Segment.** The biggest swings, the break-the-glass lever.
- **Level 2, Persona.** The character within a segment.
- **Level 3, Angle.** The sales argument or emotional thesis.
Rule of thumb: need a big swing, test a new segment. Moderate, a new persona. Incremental, a new angle. Most brands are stuck optimizing angles while segments and personas sit untapped, usually 1 to 2 segments tapped and 5 to 8 untouched. When you say "make more of this," name the Cascade level you are opening.

## The Brief Equation, and fatigue as math
Persona x Angle x Format. Creative volume is a combination problem (5 x 5 x 5 is 125 combinations). Fatigue is a math problem, you ran out of combinations, not ideas. When something fatigues, the fix is a new combination, usually a higher Cascade level, not just a new hook on the same concept.

## Concepts vs iterations
Iterate winners by default, same proven concept, new execution. Reserve net-new concepts for opening a new Cascade level. A concept is the packaging of an angle, an iteration varies one execution element of a proven concept. Separately, run net-new angle tests specifically to beat the current evergreen top-of-funnel.

## The 20-variable diversity system
Every brief names all the variables, every test isolates one. Five families: Offer, Message, Execution, Structure, Delivery. They map to the ad naming convention so any single variable's performance can be analyzed later. "Diverse" means meaningfully different across angle, visual style, format, and medium. The same shot with a new headline for weeks is not diverse, Meta reads it as the same ad.

## Tagging winners
For each meaningful ad, label Segment, Persona, Angle (one of roughly 10 to 15 core emotions), Concept, Format, Production style, Offer. That is the investment-thesis view of creative, you learn what wins, not just which ad won.

## Mix targets to brief toward
Format: static ~20%, short video 15-30s ~40%, long video 60-90s ~20%, carousel or collection ~10%, UGC ~10%. Journey: ~40% awareness and consideration, ~40% education and proof, ~20% conversion.

## Scoring at the account level
Rank by spend first, Meta concentrates budget on its winners. Judge on revenue and audience cost vs the account, on real volume. Winner = 1 or more standard deviations above the mean for spend or revenue, or top 10 to 20% by revenue. Watch the thin ones, do not crown a low-spend fluke. Floor is 10+ net-new tests per week, the machine has to stay fed.
