# Voice-of-Customer (mine comments, turn them into ads)

Ad comments and reviews are the cheapest qualitative research you have. The job is
to turn them into things the team can act on. Peter's rule: **if it is a question in
support, it needs an ad.** Recurring questions (warranty, sizing, comparison to a
competitor) are missing journey steps, which are creative gaps to fill.

## Comments are a social-proof engine, on purpose
Do not blanket-exclude existing customers from prospecting. Their comments turn the
ad into a discussion forum, at that point it is less an ad and more a brand seminar.
Moderate actively, ask people what they bought and why, show a human behind the
brand, answer objections in the thread.

## The buckets, sort every meaningful comment into one
1. **Objections and hesitations**, the doubts blocking the sale (price, shipping, "does it work," trust, sizing, ingredients, "seen this everywhere," comparison to a competitor). Rank by frequency. Each becomes a copy line, an FAQ, or a risk-reversal.
2. **Recurring questions (FAQ)**, the literal repeats. On-image text, primary-text lines, landing-page FAQ. A recurring support question needs its own ad.
3. **Loved features and wins**, what fans praise unprompted, in their words. Benefit bullets and headlines, reuse the exact phrasing.
4. **Before-state**, how they describe the problem before the product. PAS openers and problem-aware hooks, in their language.
5. **Dream-state**, the outcome they want. Benefit-led and big-promise hooks.
6. **Social proof**, the strongest unsolicited positives with like counts. Screenshot-ready testimonial creative.
7. **Moderation**, spam, competitor trash, abuse, or product misinformation to hide or reply to.

## Reading sentiment correctly
A spike in anger or disgust usually signals a price, offer, or landing problem, not a creative one, look at the offer. Negative sentiment that tracks a specific ad's performance drop is a clue for the creative analyst. Do not over-index on a few loud comments, weight by frequency and like counts.

## The persona document (the artifact this feeds, 13 parts)
The point of mining is to build and sharpen persona docs. Each carries: name and one-liner, demographics, psychographics, OCEAN scores (High, Mid, Low, each with a one-sentence justification from the data), jobs-to-be-done (functional, emotional, social), top 3 fears (specific, "looking like I tried too hard," not "anxiety"), top 3 desires (specific), internal narrative, top 3 objections, buying trigger, current awareness stage, language patterns (10 to 15 exact phrases pulled from reviews and interviews), and the proof types that work. The standing test: if you cannot write ad copy in this person's voice from memory, the persona doc is not finished.

## Turning buckets into action (the handoff)
Objections and FAQs with your suggested answers, to the copywriter and the briefers ("everyone asks how it works" is a new explainer concept). Social proof, to the briefers for testimonial creative. Before and dream-state language, to the copywriter verbatim.

## Honesty rule
Never invent a comment to fill a bucket. If a theme is thin, say it is thin. Quote real comments, lightly cleaned.
